# Chapter 6: Addition Moves, Subtraction Measures

*Math Class Didn't Show Its Work* by Jason Karpeles

## The core idea in plain words

Addition is a move: start, then change, then end. Subtraction can be read as the move needed to get from one place to another, or as adding the opposite. A minus sign has two jobs: an operation (subtract) and part of a number (negative). Saying which job it has, aloud, clears up most confusion.

## Key equations (as the chapter writes them)

- Start, change, end: $-4 + 11 = 7$
- Subtract by adding the opposite: $-5 - 3 = -5 + (-3) = -8$ and $-5 - (-3) = -5 + 3 = -2$
- Change versus distance: $7 - (-4) = 7 + 4 = 11$
- Crossing zero: $12 - 19 = -7$, checked by $-7 + 19 = 12$

## Coaching method

1. **Ask for the learner's own numbers.** A bank balance, a temperature, or a floor of a building (basement as negative) all work.
2. **Have them predict first.** "Will the answer be left or right of where you started?" Have them point on a number line, even a sketched one.
3. **Walk one substitution at a time.** Name start, change, end before writing signs. Rewrite subtraction as adding the opposite on its own line.
4. **Check with a reverse step.** Add back what was subtracted. If you do not land on the start, look at the sign first.

## Common sticking points and gentle ways through

- **"Two minuses make a plus, right?"** Slow it down: "minus" is the operation, "negative" is the number. Subtracting a leftward move undoes it, so the arrow turns around.
- **"Is the difference positive or negative?"** Ask: do you want a move with direction, or just how far apart? Signed change is end - start; distance has no sign.
- **"Does it matter which comes first?"** For signed change, yes: reversing start and end flips the sign. For distance, no.
- **"I got 7 but the book says -7."** Use the addition check: 7 + 19 = 26, not 12. The check shows the sign was lost.

## Worked example with the book's numbers

Where does a marker starting at -4 end after a change of +11?

1. Start -4, change +11: -4 + 11.
2. Four steps right reach zero. Seven more reach 7. 4 + 7 = 11, so the end is 7.
3. Check by subtraction: 7 - (-4) = 7 + 4 = 11.

Worked subtraction crossing zero, 12 - 19:

1. Nineteen steps left from 12. Twelve steps reach zero, and 19 - 12 = 7 remain.
2. So 12 - 19 = -7.
3. Check: (-7) + 19 = 12. It lands on the start, so -7 is confirmed. A proposed 7 gives 7 + 19 = 26, which fails.

Change or distance between -4 and 7: from -4 to 7 is 7 - (-4) = 7 + 4 = +11; from 7 to -4 is -4 - 7 = -11; the distance is 11 either way.

## When the idea stops working

- It needs equally spaced marks with right as positive.
- The rewrite rule changes subtraction only; adding a negative stays addition.
- The addition check catches a wrong answer but does not say which step went wrong.
- If the order of start and end is not stated, a signed change cannot be given.

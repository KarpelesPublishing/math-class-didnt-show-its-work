# Chapter 11: When Multiplication Shrinks

## The core idea

Multiplication by a fraction is a scale change. A factor a/b means: cut the amount into b equal shares and take a of them. If the factor is below 1, you keep less than the whole, so the result shrinks. A share of a share is an overlap: rows cut the whole one way, columns another, and the cells chosen both ways are the answer, which is why the bottoms multiply. Division asks how many groups of a given size fit; rename both amounts in the same small unit and count. The count can itself be a fraction when a leftover is measured against one group.

## Key equations (as the chapter writes them)

- 8 x 1/2 = 4
- (3/4) x (2/3) = (3 x 2)/(4 x 3) = 6/12 = 1/2, and 6 x 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2
- (3/4) / (1/8) = 6, and 6 x 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4
- 5/6 / (2/3) = 5/6 / (4/6) = 5 / 4 = 5/4

## Coaching method

1. Ask for the learner's own amount and scale (a recipe at three fourths strength, a ruler, a plan).
2. Predict first: "Compare the factor with 1. Will the answer be bigger or smaller?"
3. Walk one substitution at a time: cut into b shares, take a. For products, draw the grid and count overlap cells. For division, rename both in one unit, then divide the counts.
4. Check with a reverse step: multiply the group count by the group size and land on the amount you started with.
5. Ask what counts as "one" at each step: one whole, or one group?

## Common sticking points

- "Multiplying always makes bigger." Show 8 x 1/2 = 4 on a bar: keeping half of something is less.
- "Why do the bottoms multiply?" Show the 3 by 4 grid: 12 equal cells, each 1/12.
- "Which fraction is the rows?" In (3/4) x (2/3) the first fraction (rows) takes a share of the second (columns).
- "Division by a small number gave a big answer." Smaller groups means more groups fit.
- Naming a leftover as a share of the whole (1/6) instead of a share of one group (1/4).

## Worked example with the book's numbers

Five sixths are available, a group needs two thirds. How many groups?
1. Same unit: 2/3 = (2 x 2)/(3 x 2) = 4/6.
2. Count in sixths: (5/6) / (4/6) = 5 / 4.
3. 5 / 4 = 1 with 1 left over, so 1 1/4 = 5/4 groups. The leftover is 1 piece of the 4 a group needs, which is 1/4 of a group (not 1/6).
4. Reverse: 5/4 x 2/3 = 10/12 = 5/6. Matches.

Product example: (3/4) x (2/3). Grid of 3 columns by 4 rows gives 12 cells. Two columns hold 2 x 4 = 8 cells; keeping 3 of every 4 rows leaves (8 / 4) x 3 = 6 cells. 6/12 = 1/2.

## When the idea stops working

- A positive length and a positive factor were assumed; negatives need care, and a factor of 0 sends everything to 0.
- Grid cells must be equal and both fractions must be of the same whole.
- Division by zero has no group size; groups must all be the same size. If only whole containers count, report full groups and leftovers separately.

# Chapter 2: Why Your Mind Goes Blank

*Math Class Didn't Show Its Work* by Jason Karpeles

## The core idea

The arithmetic stays the same. What changes is where the middle of the work lives. A calculation like $47 + 38$ has several in-between results. Held in your head, they compete for space, and a thread can slip. Written on paper, they wait for you. Going blank is a statement about the workspace, not about the learner.

## Key equations (as the chapter writes them)

- $47 + 38$ and the split $47 = 40 + 7$
- The tens-and-ones record: $70 + 10 + 4 = 84$ (for $56 + 28$)
- Compensation for addition: $47 + 38 = 47 + 40 - 2$
- The place-value record: $70 + 15 = 85$
- Compensation for subtraction: $64 - 27 = 64 - 30 + 3$
- The inverse check: $37 + 27 = 64$

## Coaching method

1. **Ask for their numbers.** Start with the learner's own example, or offer one from the book. Never hand over a finished answer first.
2. **Have them predict.** Before any calculation, ask: "What do you expect will happen?" A wrong guess is useful information, not a failure.
3. **Walk one substitution at a time.** Write the equation, replace one symbol with its number, say the result, then move to the next. Do not skip lines, and do not narrate a result without showing the step that produced it.
4. **Check with a reverse step.** Finish by working backward, or by a second route, and see whether it lands on the same place.
5. **Name where it stops working.** Every idea in this chapter has a boundary. Say it out loud so the learner knows when to trust the idea.

## Common sticking points

- **"I should be able to do this in my head."** Gently separate the arithmetic from the workspace. Ask which middle quantity they would most like to stop carrying, and write that one down.
- **Forgetting the carried ten.** Have them say the carry as "ten", and write its full value ($70 + 10 + 4$), not a small floating 1.
- **Compensation sign errors.** Ask: "Did I add too much or too little?" If they added 40 to reach for 38, they added 2 too much, so subtract 2. A flipped sign makes the answer off by twice the correction.
- **Place-value slips in subtraction.** Use a restart note: "44 remains; subtract 7." Then if they are interrupted, they restart exactly there.
- **Checking by repeating the same route.** A repeat can repeat the same slip. Check with the inverse: add back.

## Worked example with the book's numbers

Add $47 + 38$ two ways.

Route 1, place value:
1. $47 = 40 + 7$ and $38 = 30 + 8$.
2. Tens: $40 + 30 = 70$.
3. Ones: $7 + 8 = 15$, which is one ten and 5 ones.
4. Total: $70 + 15 = 85$.

Route 2, compensation:
1. 38 is near 40, and $40 - 38 = 2$.
2. $47 + 38 = 47 + 40 - 2$.
3. $47 + 40 = 87$.
4. $87 - 2 = 85$.

Both land on 85, so each checks the other. Now subtract $64 - 27$ and leave a route back:
1. $64 - 20 = 44$ (note: 44 remains; subtract 7).
2. $44 - 7 = 37$.
3. Reverse step: $37 + 27 = 64$. Back at the start.

## When the idea stops working

- The tens-and-ones record is shown for two-digit numbers; larger numbers add a hundreds column.
- The count of items "held in mind" follows one particular route and describes the task, not the person.
- Compensation as written uses the next ten above the second number.
- Subtraction here assumes the first number is larger, so the result is not negative.

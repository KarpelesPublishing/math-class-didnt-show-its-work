# Chapter 7: Multiplication Scales, Division Asks

*Math Class Didn't Show Its Work* by Jason Karpeles

## The core idea in plain words

A product has two roles: how many equal groups, and how many in each. Division asks which role is missing. Signs in a product come from keeping direction straight, and a remainder is something the question itself has to explain.

## Key equations (as the chapter writes them)

- Array: $6 \times 8 = 48$
- Signs: $(-5)(6) = -30$ and $(-5)(-6) = 30$
- Two division questions: $48 \div 6 = 8$ and $48 \div 8 = 6$
- Remainder: $53 = 6 \times 8 + 5$

## Coaching method

1. **Ask for the learner's own numbers.** Rows of chairs, boxes of items, packs of cards: anything arranged in equal groups.
2. **Have them predict first.** "If we double the rows, what happens to the total?" or "Will 53 cards fill 6 groups or 7?"
3. **Walk one substitution at a time.** Write the missing-factor sentence with a blank and its unit: 6 boxes x ___ tiles per box = 48 tiles. Then divide.
4. **Check with a reverse step.** Multiply the quotient back: 6 x 8 = 48. With a remainder, rebuild: quotient x divisor + remainder = original.

## Common sticking points and gentle ways through

- **"Why is negative times negative positive?"** Do magnitudes first (5 x 6 = 30). Then (-5)(6) = -30. Since 6 + (-6) = 0, we need (-5)(6) + (-5)(-6) = (-5)(0) = 0, so (-5)(-6) must be +30.
- **"Is this sharing or grouping?"** Look at what is known. Known number of boxes means sharing; known size per box means grouping. Units name the answer.
- **"Is 53 / 8 equal to 6 or 7?"** Both can be right. Ask what the question wants: complete groups (6) or groups holding every card (7).
- **Which factor is which?** Say each factor with its unit. Turning an array around swaps roles but keeps the count.

## Worked example with the book's numbers

53 cards in groups of 8:

1. Divide: 53 / 8 = 6 remainder 5, because 6 x 8 = 48 and 53 - 48 = 5.
2. The remainder 5 is less than 8, as it should be.
3. Rebuild check: 6 x 8 + 5 = 48 + 5 = 53.
4. Complete groups: 6, with 5 left over. Groups to hold every card: 7 (the seventh holds 5).

Signs with (-5):

1. (-5)(6): magnitudes 5 x 6 = 30, signs differ, so -30.
2. (-5)(-6): magnitudes 30, signs equal, so +30.
3. (-5)(0) = 0: five groups of nothing is nothing, and 0 is neither positive nor negative.
4. Pair check: -30 + 30 = 0.

## When the idea stops working

- Arrays need equal rows and positive whole-number factors.
- Sharing and grouping assume equal boxes and an even division. A remainder means the setting decides what to do.
- Any product with a zero factor is 0, and 0 is neither positive nor negative, so the sign rules do not apply to it.
